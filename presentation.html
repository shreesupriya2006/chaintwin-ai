How it works

Five agents, one decision

1

Detect

Manager reports the outage: supplier, start date, duration.

2

Trace

Follows materials, factories, stock, orders, customers and SLA impact.

3

Plan

Builds several different recovery plans plus a do-nothing baseline.

4

Score

Simulates each plan on the whole network and scores six measures.

5

Approve

Manager previews, then finalises. The AI never acts alone.

Every number comes from a tested calculation engine. The assistant only explains.

Recovery plans

Never one answer

Level 1

Internal

Reallocate stock, reschedule production, prioritise customers, reroute transport.

Level 2

Backup supplier

Backup suppliers and approved substitutes when losses exceed limits.

Mixed

Hybrid

Combine internal and outside actions to balance cost and promises.

Baseline

Do nothing

Shows the damage without action, so the benefit is measurable.

Six scores per plan
CostSLARecovery timeCustomer impactOperational riskSustainability (CO₂)

What makes it different

Beyond a dashboard and a chatbot

🌊

Ripple Effect Detector

Flags when a local fix, like moving stock, starves another factory, and penalises plans that shift the problem.

🛡️

Red-Team Stress Test

Attacks the best plan with late deliveries, partial supply and longer outages to score its robustness.

🔎

Known vs Assumption

Every fact is tagged KNOWN or ASSUMPTION. Edit an assumption and plans update instantly.

⏱️

What-If and time travel

Simulate on a safe copy, then move the clock forward to watch orders and stock evolve.

Trust and guardrails

Decisions a business can audit

Facts vs assumptions

Everything on screen is tagged KNOWN or ASSUMPTION. Unconfirmed quotes and unapproved substitutes raise the plan’s risk score.

Human in the loop

Proposed, then Previewed, then Finalised. Nothing changes in live data until the manager approves, and every approval is logged with Undo.

Hard constraints first

Supplier capacity, factory limits, non-negative stock and route availability are checked before any plan is ranked.
